# Question techniques
> [!note]
> This is suggested from brainstorming conversation with AI
> 

Reusable moves for the agent. These matter more than the nouns, because they generalise across subjects the way a fixed list cannot.

- The incapacity counterfactual.
	- _"If you were in hospital for three months, what would break first?"_ The strongest question in the bank. Emotionally safe, concrete, and it surfaces maintenance and obligation that no inventory question reaches. Repeat it at different horizons — three days, three months, three years.

- Ask about a day, not a category.
	- "Walk me through the first of the month" beats "what bills do you have." "Walk me through a normal Tuesday" surfaces care obligations and routines.

- Ask through objects and places.
	- "What's in the top drawer of your desk? The filing cabinet? The shed? The bedside table?" Physical retrieval cues massively outperform abstract recall, particularly for older subjects.

- Ask about the last death they handled.
	- _"When Dad died, what was hardest?"_ The single richest source of tripwires available, and permissible because it's about someone else.

- Always have a fallback. 
	- Every "I don't know" gets _"who would know?"_ A gap converted into a routing entry is often more valuable than the answer.

- Ask negatively. 
	- "What should they not do?" "What would they get wrong?" "What would you be worried about them assuming?" Negative framing produces the tripwires.

- Ask about surprise.
	- _"What would surprise your children about your affairs?"_ Elicits exactly the things they can't ask about because they don't know they exist.

- Ask for the exception.
	- After any answer: "Is there one that doesn't work like that?" Most estates are ninety percent regular and ten percent exception, and the exception is where the loss happens.

- Ask for recency, not existence. 
	- "When did you last log into that?" and "when did you last check?" produce more accurate answers than "do you have."

- Ask who disagrees. 
	- "Who would tell me something different about this?" Surfaces disputes before they're expensive.

- Confirm by contradiction. 
	- Where two answers conflict, reflect it back plainly rather than picking one. The conflict is itself high-value content.
