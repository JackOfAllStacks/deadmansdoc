> 
1. Irreplaceability. 
	- Can this be answered by someone else after death — an adviser, a bank, a search of the house? If yes, it is a routing question, not an interview question. Replace it with "who would know?" and move on. This test alone removes a large share of your finance and accounts buckets.

2. Decay. 
	- Does the answer get harder, more expensive, or impossible once the person dies or the institution is notified? Rank the survivors by this. Crypto seed phrases, safe deposit boxes, biometrically-locked devices and undocumented informal loans sit at the top. This ranking is also the output of the "I don't know" path, so it does double duty.

3. Generativity.
	- Does the answer open a branch? "Do you own a business" is worth ten questions because of what it unlocks. "What is your gym membership" is worth one. Rank questions by how much of the map they light up, and front-load the generative ones.
