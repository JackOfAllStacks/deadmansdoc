- [ADNS](https://miro.com/app/board/uXjVHr4dBVc=/?moveToWidget=3458764683246633944&cot=14) - Australian Death Notification Service - A free centralised national service to notify corporations of a death using a single notification.
- Asymmetric disclosure currently only exists in the capacity as a security feature by technical users. The framing of it being an emotional choice, to prevent disclosure until after death, is unoccupied.
- Pre-existing competitors already come in the form of a vault or life-vault. But none have employed AI for the interview and information gathering process
- During the search for Adult-Child use cases for this scenario, there are articles about conversation starters, but no product built to serve the dyad, of two people sat at the table, working through this together. One person holding knowledge, and the other who will inherit the problem.
- Commercial model is never direct to to consumer. Competitors go through employers and advisers and insurers. Funeral directors and are free at time of use.
- Concept Note - This product is something that gets information out of the head of the parent, and decides when to and who to present vital information to in the event of a death. A tool to initiate a difficult conversation that needs doing. A patient interview process facilitated with AI help.
- DOES NOT TOUCH ANYTHING to do with distribution of assets. That is a domain left to the will and Lawyers.
- The value proposition of something physical, paper, is real. Being technological isn't guaranteed or necessary
- Undue influence. Estate lawyers actively warn against adult children helping parents complete DIY estate services, because documents produced under a child's involvement can be challenged on capacity or undue-influence grounds — the exact configuration our MVP has chosen.
- Must explicitly state NOT A WILL and has no TESTAMENTARY EFFECT
